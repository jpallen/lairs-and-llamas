# Campaign Building Guide

A step-by-step guide for creating D&D 5e campaigns for use with Lairs & Llamas.

## Phase 1: Concept

Write a single paragraph summarising the campaign. This should establish:

- **The setting** — where the campaign takes place (a city, a region, a frontier)
- **The big bad** — who or what the main antagonist is, and what makes them a compelling villain
- **The hook** — why the players are drawn in
- **The escalation** — how the threat grows over the campaign
- **The constraint** — what makes the villain hard to defeat (not just "they're powerful" but a specific mechanical or narrative challenge)

The big bad should have a **personal motivation** beyond generic evil. Why are they doing this? What do they want? What's their weakness? A villain the players can understand (even if they hate them) is more compelling than a plot device.

## Phase 2: Quest Outline

Expand the concept into a list of quests. For each quest, write:

- **Overview** — one paragraph describing what happens
- **Style** — the type of gameplay (dungeon crawl, investigation, social, heist, combat, defence, etc.)
- **Creatures** — specific monsters and NPCs involved, with approximate numbers
- **Plot hook** — what the party learns or gains, and how it connects to other quests

### Quest Count and Pacing

Scale the number of quests to the level range:

- **Early levels (1-3):** 1 quest per level. Players are establishing themselves. Keep encounters simple and use these to introduce the setting, key NPCs, and the first hints of the main threat.
- **Mid levels (4-8):** 2-3 quests per level. The conspiracy/threat deepens. Vary the gameplay style — alternate combat with investigation, social encounters, and stealth.
- **High levels (9+):** 3 quests per level. The stakes are highest. Mix large-scale battles with personal moments. The finale should be a multi-part operation, not a single fight.

A campaign spanning levels 1-10 should have roughly 19-22 quests. Levels 1-11 should have 22-25.

### Variety Checklist

Ensure the quest list includes a good spread of gameplay styles. No two consecutive quests should use the same style. Across the full campaign, aim for:

- [ ] At least 3 **investigation/mystery** quests
- [ ] At least 2 **social/roleplay** quests with minimal combat
- [ ] At least 2 **stealth/infiltration** quests
- [ ] At least 2 **dungeon crawls** with puzzle elements
- [ ] At least 2 **large-scale combat** encounters (street battles, sieges, ship assaults)
- [ ] At least 1 **defence/siege** encounter (the party protects something)
- [ ] At least 1 **heist** or timed objective
- [ ] At least 1 **moral dilemma** where there is no clear right answer

Flag any stretch of 3+ quests that are all combat-focused and break it up.

## Phase 3: Review — Story and Structure

Before detailing individual quests, review the outline for structural problems:

### Non-linearity

The campaign should not read as a strict sequence. Check for:

- **Concurrent quests.** At each level tier, are there multiple quests available simultaneously? Players should choose what to pursue, not be told what's next.
- **Multiple discovery paths.** Each quest should have 3-6 different ways the party can learn about it (different NPCs, clues from different quests, environmental discovery, overheard rumours). No quest should depend on a single trigger.
- **Branching consequences.** Key player choices should ripple forward. For each major decision point, write out what happens if the party chooses A vs B, and how that affects later quests.
- **Optional quests.** At least a third of quests should be skippable. For each optional quest, state the consequence of skipping it (lost allies, harder fights, missing intelligence). The campaign must remain completable regardless of which optional quests are done.

### Quest Discovery

Quests should be found organically, not assigned from a board. There are three discovery channels:

1. **NPC relationships.** Different NPCs feed the party different hooks. The deeper the relationship, the more they share. Map which NPCs lead to which quests — ensure no single NPC is the only path to a critical quest.

2. **Investigation breadcrumbs.** Each quest should drop clues pointing to 1-3 other quests. Map these connections to ensure there are multiple paths through the campaign, not a single chain.

3. **Antagonist escalation.** The villain reacts to the party's actions. Track how many quests the party has completed that oppose the villain, and use that count to trigger reactive events (ambushes, frame-ups, martial law, betrayals). Create an escalation table the DM can reference.

### Levelling

Use **milestone levelling** tied to story beats, not quest counts. This allows players to skip optional content without falling behind, while rewarding thorough play with better preparation rather than higher levels.

Define 1 milestone per level. Each should be achievable through multiple paths. Example: "Learn the villain's true nature" can be achieved by rescuing an NPC who knows, piecing together clues from 3+ quests, or confronting the villain directly.

## Phase 4: Review — Combat Balance

For each quest, calculate encounter difficulty using the D&D 5e encounter building rules. This catches problems early — before you've written detailed room descriptions.

### XP Threshold Table (per character)

| Level | Easy | Medium | Hard | Deadly |
|-------|------|--------|------|--------|
| 1 | 25 | 50 | 75 | 100 |
| 2 | 50 | 100 | 150 | 200 |
| 3 | 75 | 150 | 225 | 400 |
| 4 | 125 | 250 | 375 | 500 |
| 5 | 250 | 500 | 750 | 1,100 |
| 6 | 300 | 600 | 900 | 1,400 |
| 7 | 350 | 750 | 1,100 | 1,700 |
| 8 | 450 | 900 | 1,400 | 2,100 |
| 9 | 550 | 1,100 | 1,600 | 2,400 |
| 10 | 600 | 1,200 | 1,900 | 2,800 |
| 11 | 800 | 1,600 | 2,400 | 3,600 |
| 12 | 1,000 | 2,000 | 3,000 | 4,500 |

Multiply per-character thresholds by party size (typically 4) to get party thresholds.

### Encounter Multiplier

| Number of Monsters | Multiplier |
|--------------------|------------|
| 1 | x1 |
| 2 | x1.5 |
| 3-6 | x2 |
| 7-10 | x2.5 |
| 11-14 | x3 |
| 15+ | x4 |

### Calculation

1. Sum the base XP of all monsters in the encounter
2. Apply the encounter multiplier based on the total number of monsters
3. Compare the adjusted XP to the party thresholds

### Target Difficulties

| Quest Role | Target Difficulty |
|------------|-------------------|
| Random encounter / travel | Easy-Medium |
| Standard quest encounter | Medium-Hard |
| Quest climax / mini-boss | Hard |
| Dungeon boss | Hard-Deadly |
| Campaign finale | Deadly (but not more than 1.5x the threshold) |

### Common Balance Mistakes

- **Thugs (CR 1/2) at level 1-2.** Pack Tactics and Multiattack make thugs far deadlier than their CR suggests. Use bandits (CR 1/8) for early levels instead.
- **Large numbers of weak monsters.** The encounter multiplier inflates dramatically with monster count. 8 CR 1/8 bandits look weak but the x2.5 multiplier makes them a Hard encounter. If the weak monsters are truly trivial (lemures vs level 8), note that the paper difficulty overstates the actual threat.
- **Single-monster bosses.** A lone monster suffers from action economy — 4 player turns to its 1. Solo bosses below CR (party level + 3) will feel anticlimactic. Add minions, lair actions, or legendary actions to compensate.
- **Spell immunity.** Creatures immune to spells below a certain level (like rakshasas) can invalidate an entire party's toolkit. Check what spell level the party has access to at the quest's level. If the boss is immune to everything they can cast, either reduce the immunity, adjust the campaign's level range, or ensure the party has non-spell options.
- **Multi-encounter quests.** If a quest has 2-3 encounters with no long rest, assess cumulative resource drain. Each encounter may be Medium individually, but three in a row is effectively Hard-Deadly.
- **Split encounters.** If the narrative splits a fight into phases (chase then confrontation, waves of enemies), calculate each phase separately. A Deadly fight split into two Hard phases is much more survivable.

### Recording Balance

For each quest, record the adjusted difficulty alongside the creature list. This makes it easy to review and adjust later. Example format:

```
**Creatures:** 2 veterans (CR 3), 3 thugs (CR 1/2), 1 spy (CR 1).
**Adjusted difficulty:** Base 1,850 (6 monsters), multiplier x2 = 3,700 adjusted.
**Hard** for a party of 4 at level 5 (threshold 3,000 Hard / 4,400 Deadly).
```

## Phase 5: NPCs and Factions

### The Villain

Beyond the Phase 1 concept, flesh out:

- **Motivation** — specific and personal, not generic. "Reclaim my place in the fiendish court" is better than "conquer the world."
- **Methods** — how do they operate? Charm, blackmail, brute force, manipulation?
- **Weakness** — vanity, overconfidence, a specific vulnerability? This should be exploitable by clever players.
- **Presence** — the villain should appear or be referenced in quests before the finale. Players should have at least 2-3 encounters with them (social, not combat) before the final confrontation.

### Allied NPCs

For each major allied NPC, define:

- **Name, role, and personality** in 1-2 sentences
- **What they offer** — information, resources, muscle, access
- **What they want** — every NPC has their own agenda
- **When the party meets them** — the earlier, the better. NPCs who appear only when plot-critical feel like plot devices. Introduce key NPCs in Act I as minor characters and elevate them later.
- **Active involvement** — allied NPCs should accompany the party on at least 1-2 quests, not just give orders from safety. This builds attachment.

### Factions

For a political or urban campaign, define the factions:

- **Name and purpose** of each faction
- **Named leader** with a distinct personality and attitude toward the villain
- **Relationship to the party** — starts neutral, hostile, or friendly?
- **What turns them** — what evidence, favour, or event shifts their loyalty?
- **What they contribute** — if allied, what do they bring to the endgame?

The party's accumulated faction relationships should materially affect the finale. A party that cultivated allies should face an easier final act. A party that burned bridges should still be able to win, but it should be harder.

## Phase 6: Key Mechanics

If the campaign has unique mechanical elements, define them clearly:

- **How to defeat the villain** — if there's a special requirement (blessed weapon, specific ritual, closing a portal), ensure there are multiple paths to achieving it. Never hinge the campaign on a single item from a single quest.
- **Consequences table** — for each major choice point, a brief note on downstream effects
- **Escalation tracker** — if the villain reacts to the party, define the trigger thresholds and responses
- **Custom stat blocks** — for unique creatures, write them out with full stats, not just "enhanced version of X"
- **Boss fight design** — multi-phase fights need defined phase transitions (HP thresholds), legendary actions (3/round with specific options and costs), and lair actions (initiative 20 with 2-3 options). A boss with 3 phases should have enough HP to sustain all three — typically 1.5-2x the base stat block.

## Phase 7: Write Quest Details

With the structure validated, write each quest as a full location/encounter file. Follow the format used in existing campaigns (see `Orc Kidnapping/Campaign/index.md` or `Dragon of Icespire Peak/Campaign/` for examples):

- **Location description** — what the party sees on arrival
- **Connected areas** — how rooms/zones link together
- **NPC stat blocks** — in YAML format with id, name, type, description, and relevant stats
- **Skill checks and DCs** — what can be attempted and what it reveals
- **Read-aloud text** — boxed descriptions for key moments
- **Treasure and rewards** — what the party gains

Write one quest at a time. Playtest or review each before moving to the next.

## Checklist

Use this checklist before considering the campaign complete:

- [ ] One-paragraph concept with villain motivation, hook, escalation, and constraint
- [ ] Quest list with variety of gameplay styles (no 3+ consecutive same-style quests)
- [ ] Every quest has 3+ discovery paths (no single-trigger quests)
- [ ] At least a third of quests are optional with stated skip consequences
- [ ] Milestone levelling tied to story beats, not quest counts
- [ ] Every encounter has calculated adjusted difficulty recorded
- [ ] No encounter exceeds 2x the Deadly threshold without explicit mitigation (ally support, split phases, escape routes)
- [ ] The villain has personal motivation, a weakness, and appears before the finale
- [ ] All major NPCs are introduced before they become plot-critical
- [ ] All faction leaders are named with distinct personalities
- [ ] Key player choices have documented downstream consequences
- [ ] The villain's defeat has multiple paths (no single required item/quest)
- [ ] Boss fights have defined phases, legendary actions, and lair actions
- [ ] An escalation tracker defines when the villain reacts to the party
- [ ] Spell immunity and other toolkit-negating abilities are checked against the party's level
